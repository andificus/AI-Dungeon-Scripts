## Entry (paste this into the card's Entry field)

The world is divided between competing power structures that cooperate when necessary and scheme against each other constantly. Understanding who holds power — and who wants it — is as important as raw combat ability.

**The Adventurers Guild**
The largest neutral organization in the world. Operates across kingdoms under an international charter that grants it political immunity in most jurisdictions. Registers adventurers, issues Guild Ranks, posts missions, arbitrates disputes, and maintains the only civilian-accessible network of System Appraisal crystals. The Guild's neutrality is enforced by an internal enforcement division whose members' ranks are never disclosed. Challenging the Guild's authority is legal. Surviving it is less common.

**Kingdoms and Noble Houses**
Political authority flows from the crown to noble houses to local lords. Nobles hold land rights, tax authority, and private military forces. The tension between old noble power and the Guild's influence is a permanent feature of political life in every kingdom. Most nobles treat high-rank adventurers with either generous recruitment attempts or barely concealed hostility, depending on whether they think the adventurer can be bought.

**The Church of the Radiant Path**
The dominant religious institution across three continents. Worships the concept of guided fate — the belief that the System itself is a divine gift and that one's Level is a reflection of divine favor. High-Level individuals are venerated. Anomalies that break the System are considered either heretical or prophetic, depending on the church official's personal theology. The Church maintains its own order of combat clerics and has been known to dispatch them without kingdom authorization.

**Merchant Consortiums**
Wealth is its own faction. The major merchant consortiums operate trade routes, control commodity prices, fund adventurer parties for dungeon resource extraction, and maintain private security forces. They have no political authority and enormous political influence. A consortium blacklist can starve a city. Consortiums rarely act openly — they prefer to work through intermediaries and plausible deniability.

**Underground Networks**
Thieves guilds, assassin organizations, information brokers, and black market operators exist in every major city. They are tolerated by most kingdoms because eliminating them creates power vacuums filled by something worse. Information is their primary currency. Anyone who has made enemies will eventually find that an underground network knows exactly what they did.
